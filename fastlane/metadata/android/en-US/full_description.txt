Keepbook is a local-first personal finance toolkit built around data ownership.

Your accounts, balances, transactions, and price history live on your device as
plain JSON, TOML, and JSONL files. The on-disk format is human-readable and easy
to inspect or edit with ordinary tools, and the whole data directory is
portable: copy it, back it up, put it in version control, and keep it for
decades without lock-in.

Features

* Net worth and portfolio valuation across every account and asset you track.
* Portfolio history with daily, weekly, and monthly granularity.
* Asset views with trailing changes, price freshness, and holding details.
* Spending totals broken down by category over any date range.
* Multi-currency support with a configurable reporting currency.
* Manual balance entry for accounts you would rather not connect.
* Optional Git integration to version and sync your data directory yourself.

Your data stays yours

Keepbook has no account system, no telemetry, and no analytics. Nothing is
uploaded to a Keepbook server, because there is no Keepbook server. The app runs
entirely on your device against your own files.

Network access

Keepbook can optionally connect directly to financial institutions and market
data providers to refresh balances, transactions, and prices. Those services
(for example brokerages, banks, and price feeds) are operated by third parties
and are not free software, which is why this app is flagged with the NonFreeNet
anti-feature. Connections are opt-in and configured by you; if you only enter
balances by hand, Keepbook never talks to the network at all. Credentials are
stored locally and are never sent anywhere except to the institution they belong
to.

Keepbook is free software under the MIT or Apache-2.0 licenses, and also ships
as a command-line tool and a desktop application.
